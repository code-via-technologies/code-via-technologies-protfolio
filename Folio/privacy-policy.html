Privacy Policy for Folio

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Quick summary: Folio works without an account, and has no advertising. Without an account, your notes stay on your device and, if you enable iCloud, sync privately through your own Apple account. If you choose to sign in to a Folio account (to share notes or back them up), the notes and to-dos in that account are stored on servers we operate so they can sync between your devices and the people you share with. If you are a Folio Pro user and choose to connect an AI assistant (the app calls this Folio AI), the to-dos it reads are sent to that assistant's provider, a separate company that handles them under its own terms, and what it adds is saved in your account. This is optional and off until you approve it. See "Connected AI assistants". Separately, the iPhone, iPad and Mac app sends usage analytics, tied to a random install ID rather than your name: on by default, except in the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland where it asks first, and off with one tap in Settings. Nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising.

Who we are

Folio is operated by Tom Weiss, trading as Code VIA Technologies ("we", "us"), an individual developer based in Israel. We are responsible for the personal data described here (the "controller" under European data protection law, and the database controller under Israel's Privacy Protection Law). You can reach us at the email address at the end of this policy. Using Folio is also subject to our Terms of Service.

No law requires you to give us any personal data. Folio works without an account, and you can turn analytics off at any time; without an account you can't sync, share, receive shared notes or connect an AI assistant, and without sending feedback we can't answer it.

What we collect

Without a Folio account: none of your notes or to-dos. We cannot see your notes, to-dos, photos, or attachments. That content never leaves your device except through your own iCloud sync.

With a Folio account: the account information and notes described in "Folio account" below. Signing in is optional, and only notes and to-dos you keep in your Folio account library or share are stored with us.

Separately, Folio for iPhone, iPad and Mac sends usage analytics to a server we operate ourselves. This is a small, fixed set of events with no free-text fields. It never includes the content of your notes. Details are in the next section.

Usage analytics

Folio for iPhone, iPad and Mac sends on-by-default usage analytics to a server we operate. It is a short list of events, and nothing else:

  • app_open: with a flag for whether it was a cold start
  • first_launch
  • purchase: product id, transaction id, and whether it was a purchase or a restore
  • paywall_view: where the paywall was shown from
  • feature_used: the name of a feature, from a fixed list we define
  • error: an error domain and a numeric code
  • opt_out: recorded when you turn analytics off

Alongside each event we record a random ID generated on your device when you first install the app (not an Apple device identifier, and not shared with any other app), plus app version, iOS version, device model (e.g. "iPhone16,2"), language, your 2-letter country, and how many notes you currently have (a count only, never their titles or content). There are no free-text fields. Analytics never sees the text of your notes, your photos or attachments, or anything else you've typed.

The install ID doesn't carry your name or email, and we don't join analytics to your Folio account. It is still personal data, though: a purchase event carries the store's transaction ID, which also appears in your Folio Pro purchase record if you have a Folio account, so the two could be matched. App Store privacy labels list analytics as linked to you for that reason.

We do not store IP addresses in our own database or logs (the request logs our hosting provider keeps are described under "Service providers"); an IP is hashed with a key that rotates daily, used only to rate-limit abuse, and then discarded. Raw events are deleted after 90 days. A summary record per install (when it was first and last seen, app, iOS version and device model, language, country, how many times the app was opened, and whether it made a purchase) and the purchase records are kept until you ask us to delete them. Analytics never goes to an ad network or an analytics SDK. It goes to a server we operate ourselves. To keep an eye on the service, that server also posts a short alert to our private Slack workspace when a new install or a purchase is first seen, with the device model, iOS version, app version, country, and the product and whether it was a purchase or a restore. Slack stores these alerts on our behalf. Nothing is sold or shared.

In the EU, the rest of the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland (based on your device's Region setting), from Folio 1.7.0 build 212 analytics are off until you choose "Allow" when the app asks; nothing is sent and no install ID is created before that. You can turn analytics off at any time in the app's Settings. Opting out stops new events from being sent. To delete what was already collected, tap "Delete analytics data" in Settings: our server deletes the events, purchase records and install record linked to your install ID. If the server can't be reached, the app keeps retrying while it's open, and Settings shows "Retry analytics deletion" until the server confirms. You can also email us at the address below. Aggregate counts that no longer identify an install are kept. Folio for Android does not send these usage analytics; the only diagnostics from it are Google's ML Kit reports when you use Enhance with AI (see "AI features").

Folio account

A Folio account is optional. You need one to share notes, to back up your notes, and to use Folio Pro across devices. When you sign in, we store:

  • Your sign-in identity: an account identifier from the service you sign in with (Google or Apple), your name as that service provides it, and your verified email address. If you use Sign in with Apple and hide your email, we only receive Apple's relay address.
  • Notes and to-dos in your Folio account: the text, checklists, photos, files and folders of notes and to-dos you keep in your Folio account library or share, including the due dates, reminder settings and labels of to-dos, any location you add to a note and any audio or video file you attach, plus who last edited each note and a recent history of changes so your devices can sync.
  • Invitations you send: the email address you invite, an optional message, and the invitation link. We don't email the invitation; it appears in the invited person's "Shared with me" when they sign in with that address. Invitations expire after 7 days.
  • Reports and blocks: if you report a shared note or a person, we store who reported it, which note and person, the reason you picked, the details you wrote (encrypted at rest) and the note's revision, so we can review it. A report about an account is kept for up to one year after we resolve it, even if that account is deleted, so we can handle repeated abuse. If you block someone, we store that you blocked them. We also record which version of the sharing terms you accepted and when.
  • Folio Pro purchases: which store you bought from (App Store or Google Play), the product, and the store's transaction reference, so Pro works on every device you sign in to. To confirm a purchase we check it with Apple or Google.
  • Your devices: a push notification token and the platform (iOS, macOS or Android) for each device you're signed in on.
  • Connected AI assistants: a record of each connection and a content-free activity log. See "Connected AI assistants".

Account data is encrypted in transit and note content is encrypted at rest. It is not end-to-end encrypted: our server can read it in order to sync and share it. We use it to run Folio and, if you connect an AI assistant, to send it what you allow (see "Connected AI assistants"). We don't look at your notes, sell them, use them for advertising, or use them to train AI models. This applies to Folio only. An AI assistant you connect is a separate company and may use what it receives differently.

Where your data lives

  • On your device. Notes and attached photos or files are stored locally on your device.
  • In your iCloud. If you are signed in to iCloud, Folio syncs your local library through Apple's CloudKit to your private iCloud database: storage that belongs to your Apple account, protected by Apple, and inaccessible to us. Apple's handling of iCloud data is described in Apple's own privacy policy.
  • On our servers, if you use a Folio account. Your account and the notes and to-dos in it are stored in a database we operate, hosted by Railway in the European Union (the Netherlands).
  • In your Google backup, on Android. If Android backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android includes Folio's notes and attachments in your Google device backup and device-to-device transfers. Google handles that backup under your Google account; we can't see it. Your Folio sign-in is never included.
  • With an AI assistant you connect. The to-dos it reads, and anything you ask it to handle, are held by its provider under its own terms, in whichever countries that provider uses. See "International transfers" and "Connected AI assistants".

Service providers

If you use a Folio account, these companies process data on our behalf, only to provide the service:

  • Google Firebase Authentication: signing you in with Google or Apple.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple Push Notification service: delivering push notifications to your devices.
  • Railway: hosting our servers and database. Railway's network edge keeps its own technical logs of the requests it routes to our servers, which can include the time, the web address requested, the response status, and the IP address and app the request came from. Railway keeps them for a limited period that it sets, then deletes them. We don't put the content of your notes, to-dos or images in web addresses, so these logs don't contain it.
  • Apple and Google: confirming Folio Pro purchases made in their stores.
  • Google ML Kit (Android): running Enhance with AI on your phone. It sends Google diagnostics when you use the feature, as described under "AI features".
  • Slack: receiving feedback you send and the analytics alerts described under "Usage analytics", in our private workspace.

AI assistants you connect are not our service providers. They receive data at your request and act under their own terms.

International transfers

Our database is hosted in the European Union. Some of the providers above (Google, Apple, Slack and Railway) are based in the United States and may process data there. Where data protection law requires it, these transfers rely on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, as provided in each provider's data processing terms. You can ask us for more information about these safeguards.

Connected AI assistants are different. When you connect one, our servers send the to-dos it reads, and our replies to the changes it asks for, to the provider you chose. These providers are mostly based in the United States and may process data there or in other countries. They are not our service providers and we have no contract with them, so the safeguards described above do not cover that data. If you live in the EU, the rest of the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, this means your to-dos may go to a country whose laws do not protect personal data to the same standard as yours, and the provider may not be covered by an adequacy decision or by the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework: it may not be bound by equivalent rules, public authorities there may be able to access the data, and it may be harder for you to use your rights. We send the data only on your explicit permission, given in the app for that connection, and we stop when you disconnect. Read the provider's own privacy policy to see where it processes data and how it protects it.

Sharing notes

You can share a note from your Folio account by inviting someone's email address or by creating a link. Only people who accept can see and edit the note. Everyone on a shared note can see the note, the names of the other people on it, and who last edited it. You can stop sharing at any time; the people you shared with then lose access.

Connected AI assistants

Folio Pro users can connect an AI assistant, such as Claude, ChatGPT, Grok or Cursor, to their Folio account. In the app this feature is called Folio AI. This is optional. Folio works fully without it. Nothing is connected unless you approve it in the Folio app. Connecting needs a Folio account and Folio Pro. Folio AI arrives with Folio 1.9.0 and may not be switched on for your account or your version of the app yet; this section describes how it works once it is.

What is shared, and when

Folio shares data with an assistant only through the permissions you approve, and only while the connection is active. Today those permissions are:

  • See your to-dos: the assistant can receive each to-do's title, the first 500 characters of its details, due date, reminder setting, completed status, labels, its Folio ID and, if an assistant added or changed it, that assistant's name. It does not receive attachments.
  • Add and change your to-dos: the assistant sends us new to-dos and changes to existing ones, such as new text, a due date, a reminder or marking a to-do complete. We save them in your account. To-dos it adds or edits show the assistant's name. It can also attach images to a to-do (see "Images from an assistant" below).
  • Create new notes: the assistant sends us a title and text, and optionally images, and we save a new note in your account.
  • Delete your to-dos: off unless you turn it on. Only some assistants can ask for it. A to-do an assistant deletes does not go to Trash. For 7 days it can be restored, by asking a connected assistant that has this permission to restore it. There is no restore button in the Folio app. After 7 days it cannot be restored.

The assistant cannot read your existing notes. It cannot see notes that are shared with you or that you have shared. It cannot see your attachments, folders, email address, name or other account details. Images only go one way: an assistant can add an image to Folio, but Folio never sends it an image or any attachment.

Images from an assistant

If you allow it to add or change to-dos, or to create notes, the assistant can send Folio images (PNG or JPEG, up to 5 MB each) to attach to a to-do or to a new note. The image is sent to us directly, not as a link, and Folio does not download anything from the internet for it. Before we save an image we remove its metadata, such as camera details and the location where a photo was taken. We keep only the photo's rotation. The picture itself is saved as sent, so anything visible in it is saved too. Images count toward your Folio file storage, are encrypted like your other attachments, appear on your devices as ordinary attachments, and are kept and deleted like the rest of your content. Deleting the to-do or note, or your account, deletes them. An assistant cannot read an image back from Folio.

After you approve, the assistant decides when to use the permissions you gave it. You do not approve each request. You can turn any permission off when you approve, and you can disconnect at any time. Free text in a to-do can contain anything you typed, so only connect an assistant you trust with your to-dos.

Apps that Folio has not verified can only see your to-dos. They cannot add, change, delete or create anything.

Which providers

At the time of writing, assistants Folio recognises are Claude (from Anthropic), ChatGPT and Codex (from OpenAI), Grok (from xAI) and Cursor (from Anysphere). Codex is recognised only through its official connection identity. You can also connect other apps that support the same connection standard (MCP). Other local apps, such as Claude Code, are shown as "an app on this computer". Apps Folio cannot verify can only read your to-dos. Folio shows you the name and address of the app before you approve. We will update this page before we add any new assistant. Folio is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any of these companies; their names are their own trademarks.

What the provider does with it

When Folio sends data to an assistant, the assistant's provider receives it. The provider, and any AI companies it uses, handle that data under their own terms and privacy policies, not ours. They may keep it, and they may use it to train their models. Folio does not control this. We cannot see what they hold and cannot delete it for you. To see or delete it, contact the provider. Disconnecting stops new sharing. It does not remove what the provider already received. Read the provider's terms and privacy policy before you connect.

The provider is not our service provider. It is an independent company that you chose to give the data to.

What Folio stores about a connection, and for how long

We never receive or store your conversation with the assistant. We store only the following. None of these records contains the text of your to-dos or notes or any image, except the restore copy.

  • Connection record: the app's name and address, the permissions you chose, when you approved, the version of the disclosure text you accepted, and when it was last used. A connection lasts at most 90 days, then you must approve it again. We keep the record for up to 90 days after the connection ends.
  • Activity log: for each use, the time, which connection, which action (for example "add to-do" or "add image to to-do"), whether it worked, and the ID of the to-do, note or image. It never holds an image or its file name. It also holds a scrambled (keyed, one-way) fingerprint of the request. We use it for 2 minutes to stop duplicates, but it stays in the log row for the full 90 days. It does not hold titles or text. We keep the log for 90 days. The app does not show this log; you can ask us for a copy.
  • Sign-in tokens: stored only as one-way hashes. Access lasts 1 hour and renewal up to 30 days, never past the connection. They are deleted when you disconnect, and removed within a day after they expire.
  • Pending approvals: a request to connect lives for 10 minutes. We keep its record for 1 more day. It holds the app's name and address, the permissions requested, a masked form of your verified email (for example "t•••@example.com", or the last four characters of your account ID if there is no verified email) that the approval page shows as "approved by", and a one-way hash of the 6-digit confirmation code with a count of wrong attempts. Not the code itself.
  • App registrations: the name, address, redirect addresses and trust level an app gave when it registered. An app that registers itself is kept for 1 day. We keep it for as long as a connection that uses it is live, then delete it. It holds nothing about you.
  • Block list: a short list of apps we have switched off for everyone (the app's key, when, and a reason). It holds nothing about you. We keep an entry until we remove it.
  • Restore copy of a deleted to-do: if an assistant deletes a to-do, we keep an encrypted copy of it for 7 days so that it can be restored (see "Delete your to-dos" above). It is removed when restored or after 7 days.
  • Usage limits: counters of how many requests an assistant made, and how many images and bytes of images it added in the last hour, kept for 2 days. They hold no content.
  • Connection-service limits: a keyed one-way hash of the IP address (the key changes every day), to limit abuse of the connection service, kept for 1 day.

We delete expired records automatically, every minute. If you delete your Folio account, we disconnect every assistant straight away and delete all of these records. To-dos and notes an assistant added are ordinary content in your account. They are kept and deleted like the rest of it.

Our connection service does not store IP addresses and does not write the content of requests to its logs. Our hosting provider's network edge records request metadata, such as the time, the address requested, the response status and the IP address, for its own limited period, as it does for all Folio account traffic (see "Service providers").

How to disconnect

Open Folio, go to Settings, then Connected apps. Choose Disconnect next to an app, or Disconnect all. The assistant stops working straight away. You can also remove Folio in the assistant itself. If you stop having Folio Pro, connections pause, and are removed after 30 days. Disconnecting is how you withdraw the permission you gave. To delete data the provider already has, contact the provider.

Why we do this (legal basis)

Sending your to-dos to an assistant: your consent. We send data to an assistant only if you allow it. You do that in the Folio app, separately for each connection, by turning on the third-party AI permission, which is off by default, and approving. That permission is not a separate consent to sharing sensitive information, such as details about health, and it cannot give consent on behalf of other people named in your to-dos. Leave sensitive information about you or anyone else out of to-dos an AI app can read. The transfer to the provider's country is described under "International transfers". Your consent lasts until you disconnect, and at most 90 days, after which you must approve the connection again. You can withdraw it at any time by disconnecting. Withdrawing does not undo sharing that already happened.

Saving what an assistant adds: our contract with you. To-dos, notes and images an assistant adds are stored and synced as part of your Folio account, like the rest of your content. The 7-day restore copy of a to-do an assistant deletes is kept for the same reason, so that it can be restored.

Records about a connection: our legitimate interests. We keep the connection record, activity log and limits to keep the feature secure, to prevent abuse, and to be able to show what you approved. You can object by emailing us.

Notifications and Live Activities

Reminders and Lock Screen Live Activities are scheduled and shown by your device. Without a Folio account, their content never leaves your device except via your own iCloud sync. If a note or to-do is in your Folio account, its content, due date and reminder setting are stored on our servers and sync through them, like the rest of your account. That includes to-dos and reminders a connected AI assistant adds or changes: the assistant sends them to our servers, we save them in your account, and your devices then schedule the reminder themselves.

If you use a Folio account, we send push notifications through Apple and Google. When someone shares a note with you, the notification shows their name, not the note's title. Notifications that keep your devices in sync carry no note content.

AI features on your device (Enhance with AI)

"Enhance with AI" runs entirely on your device: on iPhone, iPad and Mac it uses Apple Intelligence, and on Android it uses Google's on-device Gemini Nano model, which runs through Android's AICore system service. The text of your notes is never sent to us or to any third party by this feature. It only works on devices that support these on-device models; elsewhere the app explains why it isn't available. This is separate from connecting an AI assistant, which does send data to a third party. See "Connected AI assistants".

On Android, Folio Pro users can tap Enhance with AI. When they do, Google's ML Kit library, which connects Folio to the on-device model, sends Google diagnostic information: device and app information, performance metrics, feature usage and error codes, configured languages, and identifiers. Google uses it for diagnostics and usage analytics. It never includes the text of your notes, and nothing is sent unless you use the feature.

Photos, files and locations

When you attach a photo or file, Folio receives only the item you explicitly pick. It has no access to the rest of your photo library or file system.

When you add your current location to a note, Folio asks for location access while you use the app and reads your position once, at that moment. The location is saved in the note like any other content: on your device, in your iCloud, and on our servers only if the note is in your Folio account. Folio never tracks your location in the background.

Feedback

If you use Send Feedback in the iPhone, iPad or Mac app, we receive your message with the app and system versions. It is stored on the same server as analytics and forwarded to our private Slack workspace so we can read it. Slack stores it on our behalf. Feedback is kept until you ask us to delete it; it isn't tied to your account, so tell us roughly what you wrote and when.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Trash: notes you delete from your Folio account are permanently removed from our servers 30 days after they go to Trash, or right away if you choose "Delete permanently". To-dos do not go to Trash. For a to-do that an AI assistant deletes, we keep an encrypted restore copy for 7 days, so that an assistant you allow can restore it.
  • Deleting your account: you can delete your Folio account in the app's account settings. This permanently removes the notes, folders and files you own, your invitations, purchase records, device tokens, AI assistant connections and their activity log, and removes you from notes others shared with you. Your name and email are erased; a "Deleted account" placeholder remains so the change history of notes shared with others stays consistent. We also keep a one-way digest of your sign-in ID and the deletion time, so an old sign-in can't recreate the deleted account, and reports about your account as described under "Reports and blocks". Purchases themselves stay with Apple or Google. Deleting your account does not delete what an AI provider already holds; ask the provider. Deleting your account doesn't delete usage analytics, which aren't tied to your account; you can ask us to delete those separately.
  • How long we keep data: account data and the notes in your account are kept until you delete them or your account. Invitations are removed 90 days after they expire or are answered. Device push tokens are removed when you sign out or delete your account. Resolved reports are kept for up to one year. Raw analytics events are kept for 90 days; analytics summaries, purchase records and feedback are kept until you ask us to delete them. Copies in backups are removed as those backups expire. Records about connected AI assistants are kept for the periods listed under "Connected AI assistants".

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to access the data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, give you a copy in a portable format, restrict how we use it, or stop using it (object). Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time. You can export your notes from the app yourself, and delete your account in the app. If you connected an AI assistant, you withdraw your consent by disconnecting it in Settings, then Connected apps, and you can ask us for a copy of your connection records and activity log. To access or delete data held by an AI assistant's provider, contact that provider. For anything else, email us at the address below; we reply within one month. We may need to confirm that the request comes from you.

If you're unhappy with how we handle your data, please tell us first. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, such as the supervisory authority in your EU country, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, or Israel's Privacy Protection Authority.

Children's Privacy

Folio is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has created a Folio account, contact us and we will delete it. Connecting an AI assistant needs a Folio account, so the minimum age in our Terms of Service applies to it. AI providers set their own minimum ages, which can be higher.
